NixNax

A two-player 4×4 stacked-pieces game on Midnight, settled with zero-knowledge proofs.

01

Stack to win. 12 pieces each in 4 sizes; a larger piece covers a smaller one. Four visible tops in a row wins.

02

The dice pick the move. Each turn opens with a joint roll: under 3 of 16 removes a piece, otherwise you place one.

03

Neither player controls it. The roll is the XOR of secret bits both sides committed before the game began.

NixNax

Why it is fast

A move per transaction would mean a proof and a block per move. Instead the players agree moves peer-to-peer and the chain only ever sees the result — without having to trust that it is legal.

One commitment per player

At create/join each side commits a single Merkle root: its token tree, one salted leaf per legal action (turn, kind, cell, size) — 81 per turn, depth 14, 16,384 leaves. The tree itself is never revealed.

One leaf per settled move

Settling a move reveals only that move's one-time token and its path. The circuit recomputes the leaf with persistentHash and checks it sits under your committed root using merkleTreePathRoot. Every leaf binds the gameId, so nothing replays across games.

A whole game costs createGame + joinGame + ⌈moves / 8⌉ settles + claimResult — not one transaction per turn.

createGame · joinGame

Open a game under a client-generated gameId. Each side commits its identity and its token root.

settle

Replay up to 8 agreed moves, each proving its one-time token, with every board rule re-checked in-circuit. The chain can never hold an illegal position.

claimResult

Finalise and mint one shielded nixnax:win token to the winner, who proves knowledge of a secret rather than signing.

What trust costs

The simplified contract assumes the two players do not cheat. That one assumption deletes the fraud proofs, the roll dispute, the timeouts and two of the three Merkle trees. The complete version — git tag advanced — assumes the opposite and pays for it.

The extra lessons

Equivocation slashing over the token tree; two more committed trees that make the dice provable; an optimistic roll class with a dispute protocol; timeout forfeits.

Read the simplified contract first, then the complete one to see what removing trust costs. NixNaxArena.advanced.compact is kept in the repo side by side for exactly that, and is reference-only.
